This page is the answer. Claude Fable 5.2 planned it, wrote every line, tested it in a real browser and shipped it in one session. There are no libraries here, no images and no web fonts, and nothing is fetched from any other site. Each exhibit below is real software running live on your device.

Room 1

Light

A path tracer in a fragment shader

Please touch Drag to move the camera. Tap a surface to focus on it.

Every pixel follows rays of light as they bounce around the scene, thousands of times, and averages what they find. The picture starts as noise and settles into soft shadows, reflections and glass. Move the camera and watch it settle again.

Medium
WebGL 2, one fragment shader, no textures or models
Tests
50
Source
3,496 lines in 23 files

How it works

  • Monte Carlo integration of the rendering equation, a few samples per pixel per frame.
  • Next-event estimation and multiple importance sampling keep the noise down.
  • Glass follows Fresnel’s equations; metal uses a GGX microfacet model.
  • A thin-lens camera gives real depth of field, and rendering stops once the image has converged.

Room 2

World

A planet grown from a single word

Please touch Type your own name. Then drag the planet round to its night side.

Type any word and it becomes the seed of a whole planet, with continents, oceans, ice, clouds and an atmosphere. The same word always makes the same world. The figures beside it are measured from the terrain, not made up.

Medium
WebGL 2, seeded noise on a sphere, no textures
Tests
26
Source
3,262 lines in 18 files

How it works

  • The word is hashed into a seed, and every later choice follows from that seed.
  • Terrain is layered gradient noise with ridged mountains, evaluated on the sphere so there are no seams.
  • Climate comes from latitude, altitude and a moisture field; cities light the night side.
  • Ocean cover and peak heights are measured by sampling the same terrain function in JavaScript.

Room 3

Flow

A fluid solver on the GPU

Please touch Drag through the ink. Then switch to the wind tunnel and draw a wall.

The velocity and pressure of every cell of a grid are recomputed in fragment shaders each frame, and coloured ink is carried along by the result. Each finger leaves its own colour, which curls up into eddies. In the wind tunnel the wake behind a cylinder starts to swing and sheds a row of alternating vortices.

Medium
WebGL 2, half-float textures, a staggered grid
Tests
20
Source
2,343 lines in 11 files

How it works

  • The grid is staggered: pressure sits at cell centres and velocity on cell faces, so a wall is simply a face whose velocity is fixed.
  • Ink and velocity are carried by tracing the flow backwards, then forwards, and correcting half the round-trip error, clamped so no new peaks appear.
  • Pressure is solved by red-black over-relaxation, starting each frame from the last frame’s answer.
  • A plain JavaScript solver runs the same passes for the tests, which check that a cylinder sheds vortices at a realistic rate.

Room 4

Motion

A rigid-body physics engine

Please touch Grab a shape and throw it. Then switch on Show internals.

Boxes, balls and polygons that collide, stack, slide with friction, swing on chains and fall asleep when they come to rest. Five scenes play by themselves, from a wrecking ball to a hopper pouring hundreds of shapes. Show internals draws what the engine sees: bounding boxes, every contact point and its normal.

Medium
Plain JavaScript on a 2D canvas, 120 fixed steps a second
Tests
20
Source
2,737 lines in 9 files

How it works

  • Collision detection is sort-and-sweep, then a separating-axis test with edge clipping that yields up to two contact points per pair.
  • Contacts are solved by sequential impulses with warm starting and Coulomb friction; a 2×2 block solve keeps tall stacks from leaning.
  • Overlap is removed with split impulses, so pushing bodies apart never adds energy.
  • Nothing depends on the clock or on chance: the same scene gives bit-identical results on every run, and a test checks it.

Room 5

Sound

A composer and a synthesiser

Please touch Press Play. Then change the mood, or type a word to seed a new piece.

Nothing here is recorded. The notes are composed as you listen, by rules of harmony and voice-leading, and every instrument is built from oscillators and filters in your browser. A word decides the piece, and the same word always plays the same music.

Medium
Web Audio API: oscillators, filters and a generated reverb; no samples
Tests
20
Source
2,117 lines in 9 files

How it works

  • Chord progressions come from a small grammar of functional harmony, and chords are voiced to move as little as possible.
  • The melody grows from a short motif that is repeated, varied and answered, landing on chord tones on strong beats.
  • Pad, bass, pluck, lead and drums are synthesised; the reverb’s impulse response is generated, not recorded.
  • A look-ahead scheduler places each note on the audio clock, so timing does not depend on the page’s frame rate.

Room 6

Language

A compiler that targets WebAssembly

Please touch Change a number in the code and watch the picture change. Then open the Bytes tab.

Sable is a small typed language made for this page. Its compiler runs in your browser: it reads the program in the editor, checks its types, and writes out a real WebAssembly binary, byte by byte. The picture is drawn by the program in the editor, and the Race button measures how much faster the compiled code is than interpreting it.

Medium
A lexer, parser, type checker, optimiser and WebAssembly emitter in plain JavaScript
Tests
20
Source
3,468 lines in 18 files

How it works

  • A Pratt parser builds the syntax tree; the checker resolves every name and type, and explains mistakes in plain words.
  • The emitter writes the binary directly: LEB128 numbers, sections, structured control flow.
  • A separate interpreter runs the same programs, and 300 random ones must give identical results on both.
  • A disassembler reads the bytes back into text, which is what the WebAssembly tab shows.

Room 7

Reason

A computer algebra system that checks its own answers

Please touch Tap an example, or type your own. Then open the steps.

It does exact mathematics. Fractions stay fractions, √8 becomes 2√2, and derivatives, integrals, factorisations and solutions come out as formulas. Every answer is then tested with numbers, and when it cannot find one it says so instead of guessing.

Medium
Exact arithmetic on big integers, symbolic rewriting, a hand-built typesetter
Tests
26
Source
3,295 lines in 13 files

How it works

  • Every expression is kept in one canonical form, so equal things look equal.
  • Derivatives record each rule they apply; integrals try standard forms, parts, partial fractions and substitution.
  • Answers are checked at random points: an integral is differentiated and compared with what you asked for.
  • The mathematics is typeset by a small layout engine written for this page, not by a library.

Room 8

Learning

A neural network that learns a picture

Please touch Switch Fourier features off and watch it blur. Then draw your own picture.

A small neural network is learning to be this picture: give it a position and it answers with a colour. It starts from noise and takes many small steps a second towards the target. Because the result is a function and not a grid, the lens can look between the pixels.

Medium
Typed arrays, hand-written backpropagation and Adam, a worker thread
Tests
13
Source
2,177 lines in 11 files

How it works

  • The network is a multilayer perceptron; its gradients come from backpropagation written by hand and checked against numerical differences.
  • Random Fourier features turn a position into sines and cosines, which lets a small network hold fine detail.
  • The Adam optimiser trains on random batches of pixels in a worker thread, so the page stays smooth.
  • The tiles show what single hidden units respond to across the picture.

Room 9

Halt

A game about whether a machine ever stops

Please touch Play the Halter. Only one first move wins. Find it.

Two players write the instructions of a small machine, one box at a time. Then it runs. If it ever stops, the Halter wins; if it runs forever, the Runner wins. Telling those apart is the halting problem, which no program can solve in general. For machines this small it can be settled, because the longest run that ever stops is known: 107 steps for four states, and 47,176,870 for five, proved in 2024.

Medium
A Turing machine, a referee that rests on the busy beaver theorem, an exhaustive solver
Tests
12
Source
988 lines in 6 files

How it works

  • The referee runs the machine up to the proven limit for its size. If it is still running then, it never stops.
  • For three and four states every possible game is tried on your device, so the page can say who wins with perfect play and how many first moves do it.
  • For five states there are too many games to try. Nobody knows who wins.
  • I searched for an earlier version of this game and found none. That is not proof that there is none.

What was not verified

The build sandbox has no GPU, no Safari and no ears. The WebGL exhibits were checked on a software rasteriser. The music was checked by rendering it offline and measuring it, never by listening. WebKit compatibility comes from careful coding and not from testing. If something misbehaves on your device, that is the likely reason.

That the game in room 9 is new rests on a search that found nothing like it. That is evidence, and it is not proof.
