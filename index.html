Room 1

Light

A path tracer in a fragment shader

Please touch Drag to move the camera. Tap a surface to focus on it.

Every pixel follows rays of light as they bounce around the scene, thousands of times, and averages what they find. The picture starts as noise and settles into soft shadows, reflections and glass. Move the camera and watch it settle again.

Medium
WebGL 2, one fragment shader, no textures or models
Tests
50
Source
3,483 lines in 23 files

How it works

  • Monte Carlo integration of the rendering equation, a few samples per pixel per frame.
  • Next-event estimation and multiple importance sampling keep the noise down.
  • Glass follows Fresnel’s equations; metal uses a GGX microfacet model.
  • A thin-lens camera gives real depth of field, and rendering stops once the image has converged.

Room 2

World

A planet grown from a single word

Please touch Type your own name. Then drag the planet round to its night side.

Type any word and it becomes the seed of a whole planet, with continents, oceans, ice, clouds and an atmosphere. The same word always makes the same world. The figures beside it are measured from the terrain, not made up.

Medium
WebGL 2, seeded noise on a sphere, no textures
Tests
26
Source
3,236 lines in 18 files

How it works

  • The word is hashed into a seed, and every later choice follows from that seed.
  • Terrain is layered gradient noise with ridged mountains, evaluated on the sphere so there are no seams.
  • Climate comes from latitude, altitude and a moisture field; cities light the night side.
  • Ocean cover and peak heights are measured by sampling the same terrain function in JavaScript.

Room 3

Flow

A fluid solved on a grid

Please touch Stir slowly, then quickly. Two fingers make two currents.

The fluid lives on a grid of cells, and each cell holds a velocity and a little dye. Every frame the solver carries both along the flow, then removes whatever would compress the fluid. Drag a finger through it and watch the vortices roll away.

Medium
WebGL 2, stable fluids, twenty pressure iterations
Tests
19
Source
2,149 lines in 11 files

How it works

  • Semi-Lagrangian advection keeps the simulation stable at any time step.
  • A pressure projection makes the velocity field free of divergence.
  • Vorticity confinement puts back the small swirls that the grid smooths away.
  • Every step runs in a fragment shader on half-float textures.

Room 4

Motion

A rigid-body physics engine

Please touch Knock the tower over, then rebuild it taller.

Boxes, balls and joints obey Newton’s laws here, sixty times a second. Stacks stand still instead of trembling, and chains swing without stretching. Pick anything up and throw it.

Medium
JavaScript, sequential impulses, a fixed 60 Hz step
Tests
20
Source
2,701 lines in 9 files

How it works

  • A spatial hash finds candidate pairs; the separating axis theorem tests them.
  • Contacts are solved by sequential impulses, warm-started from the frame before.
  • Stacks stay still because contact points persist from one frame to the next.
  • Joints are constraints, solved in the same pass as the contacts.

Room 5

Sound

A composer and its synthesiser

Please touch Tap play, then move the mood from calm to restless.

Nothing here is recorded. A small set of rules about harmony and rhythm writes the music one bar ahead, and a synthesiser built from oscillators and filters plays it. It does not repeat. Tap to start, then change the mood and hear it turn.

Medium
Web Audio, no samples, a look-ahead scheduler
Tests
0
Source
0 lines in 0 files

How it works

  • Chord progressions walk a graph of functional harmony.
  • Melodies follow voice-leading rules, with a seeded source of chance.
  • Every voice is synthesised from oscillators, envelopes, filters and a delay line.
  • Notes are scheduled ahead on the audio clock, so the rhythm never drifts.

Room 6

Language

A compiler that targets WebAssembly

Please touch Change a number in the code and watch the picture change. Then open the Bytes tab.

Sable is a small typed language made for this page. Its compiler runs in your browser: it reads the program on the left, checks its types, and writes out a real WebAssembly binary, byte by byte. The picture is drawn by the program in the editor, and the Race button measures how much faster the compiled code is than interpreting it.

Medium
A lexer, parser, type checker, optimiser and WebAssembly emitter in plain JavaScript
Tests
20
Source
3,457 lines in 18 files

How it works

  • A Pratt parser builds the syntax tree; the checker resolves every name and type, and explains mistakes in plain words.
  • The emitter writes the binary directly: LEB128 numbers, sections, structured control flow.
  • A separate interpreter runs the same programs, and 300 random ones must give identical results on both.
  • A disassembler reads the bytes back into text, which is what the WebAssembly tab shows.

Room 7

Reason

A computer algebra system that checks its own answers

Please touch Tap an example, or type your own. Then open the steps.

It does exact mathematics. Fractions stay fractions, √8 becomes 2√2, and derivatives, integrals, factorisations and solutions come out as formulas. Every answer is then tested with numbers, and when it cannot find one it says so instead of guessing.

Medium
Exact arithmetic on big integers, symbolic rewriting, a hand-built typesetter
Tests
23
Source
3,246 lines in 13 files

How it works

  • Every expression is kept in one canonical form, so equal things look equal.
  • Derivatives record each rule they apply; integrals try standard forms, parts, partial fractions and substitution.
  • Answers are checked at random points: an integral is differentiated and compared with what you asked for.
  • The mathematics is typeset by a small layout engine written for this page, not by a library.

Room 8

Learning

A neural network, trained as you watch

Please touch Draw something, then watch it being learned.

A small network starts out knowing nothing and is shown a picture, one pixel at a time. With every step it adjusts its weights to be a little less wrong, and the image slowly comes into focus. Draw your own picture and it will learn that instead.

Medium
JavaScript, hand-written backpropagation, Adam
Tests
0
Source
0 lines in 0 files

How it works

  • A multilayer perceptron maps a position on the canvas to a colour.
  • Fourier features let a small network hold fine detail.
  • Gradients come from reverse-mode differentiation, written by hand.
  • The Adam optimiser takes a few hundred steps a second in a worker.

What was not verified
